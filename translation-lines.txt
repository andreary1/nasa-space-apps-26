196|Barchan Dune Field
197|Classic crescent-shaped barchan dune morphology with a distinct slip face.
198|Mean wavelength (297 m) closely matches Gale dunes (~100 m) when applying Bagnold scaling β ≈ 2.82×.
199|Active migration driven by a steady unidirectional wind regime (trade winds).
200|Earth's gravity (1.0g) limits dune height to ~10 m, whereas on Mars (0.38g) dunes exceed 25 m.
201|Interdune lagoons and groundwater moisture are present, unlike on the present-day surface of Mars.
202|Clean quartz sand with high solar reflectance versus dark basaltic grains rich in pyroxene/olivine on Mars.
205|Barchanoid Dune Field
206|Regular pattern of continuous transverse barchanoid ridges similar to Nili Patera.
207|Spatial ridge density of ~6.5/km² with high geometric periodicity.
208|Strongly convergent ridge/spacing aspect ratio after gravitational scaling.
209|Hydrological regime with seasonal precipitation exceeding 1500 mm/year.
210|Localized stabilization by biofilms and coastal vegetation along the field margins.
211|Earth's dense atmosphere (1013 mbar) supports much more continuous sand transport than Mars (6 mbar).
214|Transverse Dune Field
215|Distinctive reddish/pink coloration from iron oxide coatings (hematite/goethite), identical to the spectrum of Martian sands.
216|Consistent ridge azimuth (167°) matching transverse ridges in Gale Crater.
217|Low moisture index and high aeolian mobility in a coastal semiarid environment.
218|Quartz coated with iron-rich clays versus Martian mafic basaltic sand.
219|Daily coastal sea breezes versus seasonal planetary dust storms on Mars.
220|Thicker atmospheric boundary layer on Earth.
223|Fossil Dune Field
224|Paleo-aeolian dunes with degraded ridges, analogous to fossil and lithified dune fields in ancient Martian craters.
225|Low aridity index (0.237), within the semiarid Caatinga interior.
226|Wide ridge spacing of nearly 500 m.
227|Sparse xerophytic vegetation (Caatinga) restrains active sand migration.
228|Ancient fluvial interaction with the paleochannel of Rio São Francisco.
229|No seasonal CO2 ice, which affects high-latitude Martian dunes.
230|Star Dune Mega-Erg
231|Extreme hyperaridity and monumental height (up to 45 m), compatible with Proctor and Kaiser megadunes on Mars.
232|Almost no vegetation and a surrounding desert bedrock substrate.
233|Multidirectional ridge breaks associated with variable seasonal winds.
234|Earth's atmospheric pressure sustains continuous saltation at moderate wind speeds (15 km/h), versus >80 km/h required on Mars.
235|Daytime surface temperatures reach +45°C, versus average subzero Martian temperatures (-60°C).
237|Among Earth's tallest dunes, comparable to basaltic megadunes on Mars.
238|Intense iron oxidation with a deep brick-red color identical to Martian regolith.
239|Flat interdune salt and clay surfaces (vlei) similar to Martian evaporite beds.
240|Frequent morning coastal fog (Atlantic advection fog).
241|Extremely old geological age with deep structural stabilization.
242|Gravity of 1.0g prevents the formation of meter-scale wind ripples (TARs).
